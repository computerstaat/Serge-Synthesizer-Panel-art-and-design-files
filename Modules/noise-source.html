How it works

A noise transistor produces white noise; a filter shapes it into pink noise. Both appear on their own outputs.

The S/H source feeds a sample-and-hold so that each trigger, from a pulse or the panel button, gives a new equally probable random voltage.

Bill of materials

Spreadsheet: noise-source.csv (CSV; opens in Excel, Numbers or Google Sheets). It lists the parts for every build option: Ken Stone's parts lists, and the parts data from Low-Gain's, Prism's and Elby's own BOMs where they publish one.

The makers' parts lists were copied into the spreadsheet automatically from their PDFs and Mouser carts (values, quantities and reference designators only; each row links its source). Check them against the maker's current BOM before ordering, and read the maker's notes there: they are not repeated here.

Order from Mouser: noise-source__cgs97_noise.csv (36 lines, about $18.81 at single-unit prices), noise-source__prism_cart.csv (39 lines, about $47.56 at single-unit prices). Files ending _cart are made from a maker's own Mouser cart. Upload one at Mouser's BOM tool (it reads the Mouser and manufacturer part number columns). Parts Mouser doesn't stock are listed in noise-source__cgs97_noise_not_at_mouser.csv, noise-source__prism_cart_not_at_mouser.csv.

Makers' Mouser carts: Prism Circuits Noise / Random: Prism Mouser cart (the Prism BOM). Each opens the maker's shared Mouser project, where you can add the whole list to your cart. They are kept by the makers, so they follow the current board revision, but some of their parts were out of stock or obsolete on 21 Nov 2026. The _cart upload files above are the same lists with those parts swapped for in-stock equivalents, so use them if Mouser shows a part as backordered.
