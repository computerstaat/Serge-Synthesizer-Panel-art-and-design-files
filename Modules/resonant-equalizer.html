Resonant Equalizer

RES EQ · Fixed Resonant Filterbank · CGS202

A ten-band fixed filter bank whose bands can boost, cut or ring, with two comb outputs carrying alternate bands.

How it works

The input is split across ten fixed band-pass sections. Apart from the outermost bands they sit a major seventh apart rather than an octave, so the bank does not favour any one key the way a graphic EQ does.

Each band knob is flat at its centre, boosts or cuts by roughly 12 dB either side, and past about three o'clock the band starts to resonate, so the module can ring like a set of tuned resonators when struck with pulses.

Besides the summed EQ output, two comb outputs each carry every other band; mixing or panning them gives hollow, phaser-like spectra and stereo spread from a mono source.

Bill of materials

Spreadsheet: resonant-equalizer.csv (CSV; opens in Excel, Numbers or Google Sheets). The Mouser columns are there to fill in with order links.

Order from Mouser: resonant-equalizer__cgs202_reseq.csv (42 lines, about $28.75 at single-unit prices). Upload it at Mouser's BOM tool (it reads the Mouser and manufacturer part number columns). Parts Mouser doesn't stock are listed in resonant-equalizer__cgs202_reseq_not_at_mouser.csv.

Check stock and price before ordering: 26 lines in this file (marked [UNVERIFIED] in the Description column) use part numbers built from the manufacturers' numbering and not yet checked on mouser.com; their prices are estimates. The BOM tool flags anything it can't find or that is out of stock.

Pots and knobs from Tayda: resonant-equalizer__cgs202_reseq.csv (3 lines, about $9.02). The pots (16 mm, solder lugs, 1/4" round shaft) and Davies 1900H-style knobs cost less at Tayda Electronics than at Mouser, so they are listed here with a link to each Tayda product instead of in the Mouser file.
