How it works

Each half produces a voltage that rises and falls at rates set by the RISE and FALL knobs. What it does depends only on what is patched into it.

Slew: a voltage at IN is followed by the output, but no faster than the RISE and FALL times allow. With a stepped voltage at IN this gives portamento; with the knobs fully up the output simply copies the input.

Envelope: a pulse at TRIG IN starts a rise from 0 to about +5 V followed by the fall. A new trigger during the envelope is ignored, and an END pulse comes out when the cycle finishes.

Oscillator: patch END back to TRIG IN (or set the cycle switch where fitted) and the slope runs continuously, from slow LFO up to audio. With RISE and FALL equal it makes a triangle; making one much shorter turns it into a sawtooth.

Pitch and control: 1V/OCT halves the rise and fall times for each added volt, so the slope tracks a keyboard. VC IN, through its attenuator, varies the times too, and the switch chooses whether VC acts on RISE, FALL or BOTH.

Divider: feed a pulse train into TRIG IN with a cycle a little longer than the pulse spacing, and the generator skips pulses, producing sub-harmonics.

Bill of materials

Spreadsheet: dual-universal-slope-generator.csv (CSV; opens in Excel, Numbers or Google Sheets). It lists the parts for every build option: Ken Stone's parts lists, and the parts data from Low-Gain's, Prism's and Elby's own BOMs where they publish one.

The makers' parts lists were copied into the spreadsheet automatically from their PDFs and Mouser carts (values, quantities and reference designators only; each row links its source). Check them against the maker's current BOM before ordering, and read the maker's notes there: they are not repeated here.

Order from Mouser: dual-universal-slope-generator__cgs114_dusg.csv (34 lines, about $53.55 at single-unit prices). Files ending _cart are made from a maker's own Mouser cart. Upload one at Mouser's BOM tool (it reads the Mouser and manufacturer part number columns). Parts Mouser doesn't stock are listed in dual-universal-slope-generator__cgs114_dusg_not_at_mouser.csv.

Pots and knobs from Tayda: dual-universal-slope-generator__cgs114_dusg.csv (2 lines, about $4.86). The pots (16 mm, solder lugs, 1/4" round shaft) and Davies 1900H-style knobs cost less at Tayda Electronics than at Mouser, so they are listed here with a link to each Tayda product instead of in the Mouser file.

Or genuine Davies knobs from Mouser: the Tayda knob is a Davies 1900H clone ($0.42). If you want the real Davies Molding 1900-H (Mouser 5164-1900-H, about $4.36 each; 5164-1900-L has an indicator line), upload knobs/dual-universal-slope-generator__cgs114_dusg.csv (6 knobs) at Mouser's BOM tool instead and leave the knob line out of the Tayda order.
