How it works

Each half produces a voltage that rises and falls at rates set by the RISE and FALL knobs. What it does depends only on what is patched into it.

Slew: a voltage at IN is followed by the output, but no faster than the RISE and FALL times allow. With a stepped voltage at IN this gives portamento; with the knobs fully up the output simply copies the input.

Envelope: a pulse at TRIG IN starts a rise from 0 to about +5 V followed by the fall. A new trigger during the envelope is ignored, and an END pulse comes out when the cycle finishes.

Oscillator: patch END back to TRIG IN (or set the cycle switch where fitted) and the slope runs continuously, from slow LFO up to audio. With RISE and FALL equal it makes a triangle; making one much shorter turns it into a sawtooth.

Pitch and control: 1V/OCT halves the rise and fall times for each added volt, so the slope tracks a keyboard. VC IN, through its attenuator, varies the times too, and the switch chooses whether VC acts on RISE, FALL or BOTH.

Divider: feed a pulse train into TRIG IN with a cycle a little longer than the pulse spacing, and the generator skips pulses, producing sub-harmonics.
