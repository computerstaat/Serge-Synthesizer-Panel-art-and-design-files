Infinite Melody

How it works

Each clock gives a new stepped voltage. In 1/f mode the size of a step is inversely related to how often it happens, which gives lines that wander like a melody rather than jumping at random; random mode has no weighting.

Knobs and inputs set the span and root, and the diatonic option (with Ken Stone's CGS41) keeps the output on a major or minor scale.

Bill of materials

Spreadsheet: infinite-melody.csv (CSV; opens in Excel, Numbers or Google Sheets). It lists the parts for every build option: Ken Stone's parts lists, and the parts data from Low-Gain's, Prism's and Elby's own BOMs where they publish one.

Order from Mouser: infinite-melody__elbykit_cgs32.csv (29 lines, about $36.80 at single-unit prices). Files ending _cart are made from a maker's own Mouser cart. Upload one at Mouser's BOM tool (it reads the Mouser and manufacturer part number columns). Parts Mouser doesn't stock are listed in infinite-melody__elbykit_cgs32_not_at_mouser.csv.

Check stock and price before ordering: 5 lines in this file (marked [UNVERIFIED] in the Description column) use part numbers built from the manufacturers' numbering and not yet checked on mouser.com; their prices are estimates. The BOM tool flags anything it can't find or that is out of stock.

Pots and knobs from Tayda: infinite-melody__elbykit_cgs32.csv (1 lines, about $4.20). The pots (16 mm, solder lugs, 1/4" round shaft) and Davies 1900H-style knobs cost less at Tayda Electronics than at Mouser, so they are listed here with a link to each Tayda product instead of in the Mouser file.

Or genuine Davies knobs from Mouser: the Tayda knob is a Davies 1900H clone ($0.42). If you want the real Davies Molding 1900-H (Mouser 5164-1900-H, about $4.36 each; 5164-1900-L has an indicator line), upload knobs/infinite-melody__elbykit_cgs32.csv (10 knobs) at Mouser's BOM tool instead and leave the knob line out of the Tayda order.
