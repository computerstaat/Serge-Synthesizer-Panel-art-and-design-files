How it works

A filter changes timbre by removing frequencies. The Wave Multipliers do the opposite: they bend the waveform so that new, harmonically related overtones appear. Each of the three sections works in a different way, and each has its own control voltage and knob. In a VCO-VCF-VCA patch the usual place for it is between the oscillator and the filter.

Top section: a switch picks the mode. In HI it squares up the signal with rounded, saturated peaks, like an overdriven tube amplifier but voltage controlled. In LO it is a plain linear VCA, handy for amplitude modulation or for gating a signal into the other sections.

Middle section: fed with a sine wave, turning it up sweeps through the odd harmonics, up to about the 13th. It sounds like overblowing a pipe closed at one end, so it suits reed and wind sounds. A second input lets two signals be mixed before processing, which gives sum-and-difference-like effects richer than a ring modulator.

Bottom section: chained full-wave rectifiers, each of which doubles the frequency of a sine or triangle. Sweeping the control moves smoothly through the even harmonics (2nd, 4th, 8th) without the drop in level a plain rectifier gives. It has two inputs and two outputs; the second output is a squared-up version of the first and behaves like a more complex pulse-width modulation.

The makers' parts lists were copied into the spreadsheet automatically from their PDFs and Mouser carts (values, quantities and reference designators only; each row links its source). Check them against the maker's current BOM before ordering, and read the maker's notes there: they are not repeated here.
