How it works

The input runs through a chain of all-pass stages. An all-pass stage leaves the level of every frequency alone but delays its phase, and the amount of delay depends on frequency and on the control voltage. Taps along the chain give outputs shifted by 360°, 720° and 1080°.

On their own the shifted outputs sound almost the same as the input, because the ear hardly hears phase. The effect appears when a shifted signal is mixed back with the original: frequencies that come out of phase cancel, cutting a row of notches into the spectrum. The MIXED output does this inside the module with the 1080° tap, and the MIX knob sets the balance.

PHASE sets the shift by hand. The VC input, through its attenuator, sweeps it from an envelope, LFO or sequencer; the response follows a log curve so sweeps sound even across the range.

Ken Stone's CGS90 puts two small audio mixers on the same PCB. Each has three inputs with level knobs plus one unity-gain x1 input. They are inverting and AC coupled, which makes them good for blending the raw phase outputs with the dry signal (straight or inverted) and for feedback patches: mix the phaser's output with its input before it goes back in. Patch one mixer's output into the other's x1 input to get a single six-input mixer.

Two main boards: V1 uses three dual-element 5C3/2 vactrols with flying wires; V2 (LGE090N) uses six single NSL-32 vactrols and the LGE090C I/O board. Low-Gain says the two can sweep and respond to CV a little differently.

The LGE090 phaser has an input level knob, a CV attenuator and a feedback (FDBK) knob, and each mixer has three variable inputs plus one fixed one. The Serge phaser panels in this repo have no feedback or input level control, so check the I/O board against the CGS90 Phaser with Mixers (4") panel before drilling.

The mixers are AC-coupled and inverting, so they are for audio rather than control voltages.

Low-Gain boards set their own jack positions; check them against the panel you use.

Bill of materials

Spreadsheet: vc-phaser.csv (CSV; opens in Excel, Numbers or Google Sheets). It lists the parts for every build option: Ken Stone's parts lists, and the parts data from Low-Gain's, Prism's and Elby's own BOMs where they publish one.

The makers' parts lists were copied into the spreadsheet automatically from their PDFs and Mouser carts (values, quantities and reference designators only; each row links its source). Check them against the maker's current BOM before ordering, and read the maker's notes there: they are not repeated here.

Order from Mouser: vc-phaser__cgs90_phaser.csv (21 lines, about $18.84 at single-unit prices), vc-phaser__prism_cart.csv (22 lines, about $63.94 at single-unit prices). Files ending _cart are made from a maker's own Mouser cart. Upload one at Mouser's BOM tool (it reads the Mouser and manufacturer part number columns). Parts Mouser doesn't stock are listed in vc-phaser__cgs90_phaser_not_at_mouser.csv, vc-phaser__prism_cart_not_at_mouser.csv.

Makers' Mouser carts: Prism Circuits VC Phaser: Prism Mouser cart (the Prism BOM). Each opens the maker's shared Mouser project, where you can add the whole list to your cart. They are kept by the makers, so they follow the current board revision.

Pots and knobs from Tayda: vc-phaser__cgs90_phaser.csv (1 lines, about $1.26). The pots (16 mm, solder lugs, 1/4" round shaft) and Davies 1900H-style knobs cost less at Tayda Electronics than at Mouser, so they are listed here with a link to each Tayda product instead of in the Mouser file.

Or genuine Davies knobs from Mouser: the Tayda knob is a Davies 1900H clone ($0.42). If you want the real Davies Molding 1900-H (Mouser 5164-1900-H, about $4.36 each; 5164-1900-L has an indicator line), upload knobs/vc-phaser__cgs90_phaser.csv (3 knobs) at Mouser's BOM tool instead and leave the knob line out of the Tayda order.
