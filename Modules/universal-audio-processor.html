How it works

Inside are two voltage-controlled amplifiers and a small control circuit that drives them. The control circuit makes two complementary control signals: as one VCA opens, the other closes. Because the curve is equal-power rather than linear, the overall loudness stays the same as a sound moves between the two sides, with no dip in the middle.

The three-position switch changes how the audio is routed between the two VCAs, so one panel does three jobs without any patching:

PAN (up): one input signal feeds both VCAs. PAN / FADE and VC PAN move it between OUT 1 and OUT 2: a stereo panner. GAIN and VC 1+2 set the overall level. The arrows on the panel show which input is shared in each mode.

DUAL VCA (centre): the two VCAs work on their own. IN 1 goes to OUT 1 under VC 1, IN 2 to OUT 2 under VC 2: two separate amplifiers for envelopes, tremolo or gating.

XFADE (down): two different signals at IN 1 and IN 2 are blended under voltage control. PAN / FADE and VC PAN set the mix, and as one source rises the other falls.

AM 1 and AM 2 are AC-coupled inputs for amplitude modulation at audio rates, separate from the DC-coupled VC inputs. Each output appears on both a 1/4" jack and a banana.

  • LGE206 Dual VCA: The two VCAs: OTA-based, AC-coupled buffered inputs, with offset and gain trims for calibration.
  • LGE207 Stereo Equal Power Driver: Makes the complementary control currents for panning and crossfading from the knobs, VC inputs and offset references.
  • LGE208 I/O board: Front-panel board: jacks, knobs and the mode switch; it does the routing between the two VCAs.

As of 20 Nov 2026 the build documents are listed on the product page but not all linked yet, and build notes / calibration are marked as coming soon. One link on that page opens an LGE214c Dual Exponential VCA file instead.

The LGE208 I/O board sets the jack and knob positions. The panels on this page are drawn from the Serge layouts, so check the hole positions against the I/O board before using one with the Low-Gain boards.

The CGS102 converts and sums control voltages only; it needs CGS108 gain cells for the audio path, and those need a host board.

Ken Stone does not publish the CGS108 gain cell schematic: the circuit is proprietary. His page gives the PCB overlay and parts only.

Ken Stone's Serge-derived designs may be built for personal use only; Serge Tcherepnin keeps all rights to his circuits. Schematics, parts lists and PCBs belong to their designers and sellers, so these pages link to them rather than copying them.
