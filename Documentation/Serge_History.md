# Serge history, as it bears on the panel artwork

A summary in our own words, from the Wikipedia "Serge synthesizer" article (current and older revisions) and the
Egres fan site (serge-fans.com, Darrel Johansen, Gary Chang and Kevin Braheny Fortune's pages).

## Timeline

- **1972–73:** Serge Tcherepnin, Rich Gold and Randy Cohen design the first modules at CalArts. Students pay for
  parts, build modules in an informal "assembly line" and take home a six-panel system. The CME system at UC San
  Diego, La Jolla dates from 1973.
- **1974–75:** Serge leaves teaching. Serge Modular Music Systems starts in Hollywood (Western Avenue and Santa
  Monica Boulevard).
- **Around 1976:** a new generation of modules (Quad VCA, UPAP, Precision VCO, NTO, the variable Q, slope and
  bandwidth filters, Wave Multipliers, DUSG/DTG, TKB, Extended ADSR, Pitch and Envelope Follower).
- **Around 1979:** the "Series 79" standard system; the next generation (Active Processor, Resonant Equalizer,
  sequencer programmers, comparators, Wilson Analog Delay, Quantizer, Frequency Shifter, Quadrature Oscillator, Dual
  VCA). The company moves to Haight Street, San Francisco, around 1980.
- **Mid 1980s:** business slows; the N-Voice Controller (around 1982) is about the last new module.
- **1992:** Serge returns to France and sells the designs to Rex Probe, who founds Sound Transform Systems (STS).
  STS adds Pulse Divider, Boolean Logic, an audio mixer with phase switch and other modules, and redesigns many
  layouts to be more compact.
- **Later:** licensed and DIY versions from CGS (Ken Stone), Random*Source, ELBY, Loudest Warning, 73-75 and
  others.

## How the panels were made

Every Serge panel is the same 17" × 7" pre-drilled aluminium panel holding 16" of modules. What changed is the
graphics:

| Period | Graphics | Look | Repo folder |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1973–76 | paper over the panel, lacquered; later adhesive paper with a Mylar overlay | geometric symbols for signal types, 1" grid, six rows, bigger knobs | `Paperface/` |
| about 1976–79 | adhesive paper with Mylar overlay | function names, outputs always boxed, squared boxes | `SMMS/Paper/` |
| about 1979–early 1990s | adhesive metallic sheet with the artwork photo-exposed onto it, per module or a whole panel at once | rounded boxes; normally black on silver, sometimes reversed to silver on black | `SMMS/Metal_Overlay/`, layouts in `Modern/` |
| 1992 on (STS) | printed directly on the metal | rounded boxes, many layouts made more compact | `Modern/` |

Other details:
- Early panels had every hole punched; graphics covered the unused ones.
- Jack colours: red for triggers and logic, blue for control, black for audio, yellow or green for special.
- Early Sequencers had two jacks per output, later ones one. Some early Noise Sources had only the white, pink and
  S/H source outputs.
- Darrel Johansen's custom six-panel system, built in the early 1980s with reversed (silver on black) metallic
  graphics, was donated to CalArts in 1997. That fits the Cal Arts Black Serge (`../Systems/CalArts_Black_Serge.md`).
